CHAPTER FOUR: THREATS OF THE NEW DARK AGE

This chapter presents the major threats of Edgeheart alongside their adversaries, environments, and special mechanics.

ADVERSARY FACTIONS

The New Dark Age is not ruled by one empire. It is divided between corporations, gangs, mercenary crews, corrupted machines, rogue AI, black clinics, forgotten war platforms, and desperate people trying to survive what the world became.

The factions presented here are more than enemies to defeat. Each represents a different kind of pressure in the New Dark Age.

Street Gangs

Street gangs, crime syndicates, chrome cults, protection crews, and neighborhood warbands control what corporations cannot efficiently own.

Gang adversaries are ideal for street-level contracts, territory wars, nightclub violence, black-market disputes, kidnappings, and jobs where reputation matters as much as bullets.

Corporate Forces

Corporations use private security, tactical response teams, combat drones, proxy soldiers, executives, lawyers, and deniable black-ops units to protect their assets.

Corporate adversaries work best in infiltration, sabotage, extraction, espionage, data theft, blacksite raids, and contracts where the crew is fighting money with guns.

Mercenary Crews

Mercenaries are not a faction, but a profession. Some are desperate edgerunners. Some are professionals. Some are legends. Some are exactly like the PCs, just standing on the other side of the contract.

Mercenary adversaries are ideal for rival crews, bounty hunters, contract complications, tactical fights, recurring enemies, and missions where both sides have an objective beyond survival.

Digital Ghosts

Digital Ghosts are corrupted AI fragments, haunted daemons, broken neural patterns, and machine minds that learned to manifest themselves through devices, sensors, AR overlays, speakers, implants, screens, and corrupted networks.

They are technological threats, but they often appear like ghosts, monsters, angels, saints, static corpses, childhood memories, or impossible things seen through broken code.

Use Digital Ghosts when you want horror, unreality, hallucinated battlefields, Haunted Zones, cursed technology, or enemies that feel almost magical while remaining rooted in machines.

Eidolons and Legendary Threats

Eidolons, legendary mercenaries, God-Class AI, Blackwall entities, and war machines are not common enemies. They are events.

These adversaries are best used as major bosses, campaign pivots, battlefield escalations, or signs that a contract has become much larger than the crew was told.

OVERCLOCKED ADVERSARIES

Some enemies possess dangerous enhancements from cyberware, Redline systems, AI corruption, corporate prototypes, gang rituals, or Eidolon-linked hardware.

An Overclocked Adversary gains an Overclock Trait, an Overclock Protocol, and Overguard.

Overclock Traits

Unless otherwise stated, each trait can only trigger once per scene, or if the GM spends a Fear to activate it again. You may freely assign one Overclock Trait to any adversary. The GM can also spend a Fear to add more Traits to an adversary.

  • Adaptive Plating: The first time this adversary would mark HP or Stress, ignore it.

  • Reactive Thrusters: Once per scene, immediately reposition up to Far range after taking damage.

  • Overcharged Systems: The first attack this adversary makes in a scene gains +1 Damage Die.

  • Combat Reflexes: Once per scene, after a PC damages this adversary, immediately make a standard attack.

  • Regeneration Module: At the start of this adversary’s Spotlight, if it hasn’t taken damage since its last action, clear a Stress or a HP.

  • Explosive Death: When defeated, all creatures within Close range take 1d20 techno or impact damage.

  • Phase Shift: Once per scene, become Hidden until the end of this adversary’s next action.

  • Suppressive Fire: Creatures damaged by this adversary cannot gain Hope before this adversary’s next action.

  • Null Field: PCs within Close range cannot benefit from advantage.

Overclock Protocols

Some Overclocked adversaries run dangerous combat protocols through cyberware, squad networks, tactical firmware, Redline systems, command uplinks, or corrupted machine logic.

When an Overclocked adversary takes the Spotlight, the GM may activate one Overclock Protocol until that adversary’s next Spotlight. Only one Overclock Protocol can be active at a time.

  • Command Uplink: Allies within Close range gain +1 Difficulty.

  • Assault Firmware: Allies deal +1d6 damage.

  • Signal Jammer: PCs entering Close range mark a Stress.

  • Redline Push: Allies clear a Stress when they deal damage.

  • Threat Marking: PCs within Close range cannot clear Stress.

Overguard

This adversary begins each scene with Overguard. While it has Overguard, it is immune to conditions, Major damage, and Severe damage. The first HP it would mark instead removes its Overguard.
