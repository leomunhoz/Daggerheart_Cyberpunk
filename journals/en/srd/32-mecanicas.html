CHAPTER THREE: EDGEHEART MECHANICS

The following mechanics are used in this campaign.

HUMANITY AND CYBERPSYCHOSIS

Chrome makes you faster, stronger, sharper, harder to kill. It replaces weakness with hardware, instinct with software, and pain with clean signal. But every implant leaves a mark.

Cyberpsychosis is not simple madness. It is the moment when your body, mind, chrome, memories, combat instincts, and machine systems stop agreeing on what you are.

Humanity Die

Your connection to yourself is represented by your Humanity Die. You begin with a d6 Humanity Die.

Your Humanity Die may advance when you form a meaningful bond, achieve an important background goal, reclaim part of yourself, or experience a moment of genuine hope.

You can advance your Humanity Die only once per level, in the following steps: d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12.

Cyber Load

Every Cyberware has a Cyber Cost. When you install Cyberware, add its Cyber Cost to your Cyber Load.

Your Cyber Load measures how much chrome, signal, neural pressure, and machine logic your body is carrying. Some effects can temporarily increase your Cyber Load.

Humanity Roll

When your body or mind is pushed too far, make a Humanity Roll: roll your Humanity Die.

  • If the result is higher than your Cyber Load, you remain in control.

  • If the result is equal to or lower than your Cyber Load, you enter Cyberpsychosis.

You must make a Humanity Roll when you roll with Fear while all your Stress is marked, or while you have no Hope. The GM may also call for a Humanity Roll as a golden opportunity.

While in Cyberpsychosis

  • You no longer roll a Hope Die. Instead, use only your Fear Die as a d20 and add the appropriate trait. The roll is always treated as a roll with Fear.

  • Any feature that would normally require you to mark Stress can be used without marking Stress.

  • You still control your character, but your actions should become more violent, obsessive, detached, impulsive, predatory, or machine-like. Cyberpsychosis does not take your character away from you. It changes what feels natural to them.

Stabilizing

Another PC within Close range may spend a Hope to help you stabilize. They might call your name, remind you of an Anchor, physically restrain you, cut the hostile signal, or force your systems to reboot. When they do, roll your Humanity Die again. If the result is higher than your Cyber Load, Cyberpsychosis ends. If the result is equal to or lower than your Cyber Load, Cyberpsychosis continues.

The GM may also allow Cyberpsychosis to end when you are removed from danger, disconnected from the source of overload, restrained by allies, or brought back through a powerful emotional Anchor.

Lost to Cyberpsychosis

If you end a scene while still in Cyberpsychosis, reduce your Humanity Die by one step. If your Humanity Die would be reduced below d4, you are lost. Take the Death Move: Cyberpsycho.

When you are lost to Cyberpsychosis, choose one:

  • You disappear into the city as a violent urban legend.

  • Your crew is forced to put you down.

  • You burn out in one final act of destruction, saving someone or completing the job at terrible cost.

Then create a new character.

On the Edgeheart Sheet, the automatic Humanity Roll, the Cyberpsychosis rules, the Stabilize button, and the end-of-scene reduction are already automated (see the "Welcome to Edgeheart" journal).

HACKING

Hacking is any attempt to interfere with connected technology, digital security, or automated systems. You don’t need to be a Runner to hack, but you need a plausible way to interface with the target, such as a stolen access shard, connected device, or direct physical access.

Most hacks use an Interface Roll. Unless a feature or card says otherwise, an Interface Roll usually uses Knowledge.

Use these difficulties as a general guide:

DifficultySystem
10Civilian device, public terminal, simple camera
13Gang security, basic cyberware, common drone
16Corporate security, protected server, military lock
19Eidolon hardware, advanced ICE, elite network
21+Blackwall fragment, rogue AI, expert Runner

When you succeed on an Interface Roll to hack a connected system, you create a Breach. A Breach is a temporary opening in a system. When you create a Breach, spend it immediately to choose one Hack:

  • Open/Lock: Open, close, lock, unlock, or disable a simple electronic system.

  • Loop/Spoof: Create a false feed, camera loop, fake ID, sensor mask, or spoofed signal until your next Spotlight.

  • Pull Data: Ask the GM one basic question about the system, location, map, security, connected device, patrol, or target.

  • Jam: A connected target or device marks 1 Stress or has disadvantage on its next roll that relies on the jammed system.

  • Expose: The next action roll made against the hacked target or system gains a +2 bonus.

  • Command: Briefly activate, deactivate, redirect, or control a connected device until your next Spotlight.

Hacking can interfere with technology, but it can’t fully control a living creature or solve an entire mission. Offline, analog, isolated, or purely mechanical systems can’t be hacked unless you first connect, alter, or physically access them.
