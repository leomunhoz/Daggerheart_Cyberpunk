CHAPTER ONE: PLAYING IN THE NEW DARK AGE

In this chapter, we’ll cover what you need to know as a player to start your contract, including the core mechanics, combat, advancement, equipment, cyberware, vehicles, Eidolons, and a few optional rules you can add to your game.

Edgeheart is a game about mercenaries trying to survive in a world that already ended.

You take jobs, make enemies, install chrome, uncover old secrets, run from consequences, and decide how much of yourself you are willing to sell for power.

FLOW OF THE GAME AND CONTRACTS

In Edgeheart, gameplay is centered around completing Contracts and advancing Campaign Threads.

A Contract is a short operation with a clear objective and immediate reward. Most Contracts can be completed within one or two sessions and represent the routine work of a mercenary crew in the New Dark Age.

Contracts can involve eliminating a target, stealing corporate data, escorting a convoy, recovering lost technology, infiltrating a secure facility, protecting a neighborhood, sabotaging infrastructure, hunting a rogue AI fragment, or surviving a job that was never supposed to be fair.

Each Contract should have a clear goal, a reward, and an obvious reason why the crew was hired instead of someone cleaner, cheaper, or more disposable.

Completing Contracts grants credits to buy new equipment, loot, cyberware access, vehicle upgrades, Eidolon parts, faction leverage, and other rewards that improve a character’s capabilities. These rewards are one of the main ways characters expand their options and prepare for greater threats.

A Campaign Thread represents a larger objective with significant narrative impact. Campaign Threads often require multiple Contracts to complete and usually involve powerful corporations, major factions, rogue AI, Eidolons, Blackwall secrets, personal vendettas, or events that affect an entire district or region.

The outcome of completed Contracts influences the direction of Campaign Threads. Success may reveal allies, resources, leverage, and new opportunities. Failure may strengthen enemies, expose the crew, raise retaliation, or make future jobs more dangerous.

Between Contracts, characters return to a safehouse, garage, clinic, convoy camp, gang territory, corporate apartment, black-market hub, or another temporary place of safety. This is the primary downtime phase of the game.

During downtime, players can install cyberware, repair vehicles, restore Eidolons, change equipment, recover, pursue personal goals, follow leads, manage contacts, negotiate with factions, and prepare for future deployments.

While Edgeheart focuses on action, combat, infiltration, chrome, and high-risk contracts, downtime remains an important opportunity for character development, relationships, faction pressure, and the stories that exist between jobs.

WAYS TO PLAY

Edgeheart can be played in two main ways, or a mix of both.

Driven by Story: Edgeheart can focus each Contract directly on developing the crew’s story, personal goals, faction relationships, corporate secrets, the Blackwall, the Theoi, and the truth behind the Collapse.

This style works well when the table wants mystery, recurring villains, personal stakes, and long-term consequences. Each Contract should reveal something new: a secret, a betrayal, a faction move, a piece of forbidden technology, or a clue about what really happened to the world.

Contract of the Week: Edgeheart can also be played in a "job of the week" format. The story and long-term development take a lighter role, and the main focus is on completing a different, fun, and unique Contract each session.

One week might be a convoy ambush. The next might be a stealth extraction, a street race, a black-clinic defense, an Eidolon battlefield, a gang negotiation, or a Blackwall containment breach.

You can use this format when a player is absent, when the group wants a simpler session, or when the table needs a break before moving to the next major Campaign Thread.

Most Edgeheart campaigns work best somewhere between these two styles: each Contract is exciting on its own, but the consequences slowly build into something larger.

THE INCITING INCIDENT

Maybe you fight for credits. Maybe for revenge. Maybe for freedom. Maybe because chrome is expensive and survival costs more every year. Whatever your reason, your crew has taken a Contract that should have been simple.

It should.

Someone lied about the objective. Someone else wants the same prize. The job touches a secret older than the Collapse, and something behind the Blackwall has started to notice.

The New Dark Age does not need heroes. It needs people desperate enough to go where the corps cannot admit they are looking.
