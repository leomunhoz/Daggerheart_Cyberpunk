In the New Dark Age, a vehicle is more than transportation. It is weapon, status, and sometimes the reason a crew survives.

Vehicles are treated as special Equipment that can be used while driving, riding, or operating them. A vehicle attack might represent a ram, mounted weapon, or high-speed impact.

Unless stated otherwise, a vehicle occupies the same narrative space as its driver and passengers, cannot be separately targeted like a creature, and acts as part of the driver’s movement.

A vehicle can only be used when the fiction allows it. A bike can tear through an alley and an armored rig can dominate a highway, but none of them fit inside a sealed elevator.

VEHICLE DAMAGE

Vehicles don’t track HP. When an attack or crash would seriously damage a vehicle, the driver may mark a Stress to keep the vehicle functioning or it becomes Damaged. While a vehicle is Damaged, its Feature can’t be used, and rolls made to drive or maneuver it take a −2 penalty.

If a Damaged vehicle would become Damaged again, it becomes Disabled instead and can’t move or be used until repaired. It can be repaired during a rest or through a feature such as Jury-Rig.

The GM should avoid permanently destroying a character’s signature vehicle unless the table agrees that it fits the story.

VEHICLE BURDENS

Vehicle Burdens don’t interfere with which weapons you carry, but describe how the vehicle changes movement and combat.

  • Swift: A fast and agile vehicle. While the vehicle is moving at speed, gain +1 Evasion.

  • Heavy: A bulky, reinforced, or difficult-to-maneuver vehicle. While using it, take −1 Evasion, but gain +2 Damage Thresholds.

  • Armored: A protected combat vehicle. Once per scene, when you or an ally inside it would mark HP, reduce the severity by 1.

  • Off-Road: A vehicle built for bad roads. Ignore difficult terrain caused by broken ground, rubble, or unstable streets.

  • Cargo: A crew vehicle with storage, tools, or supplies. Once per rest, reveal a useful piece of equipment stored inside.

  • Connected: A networked vehicle with autopilot, targeting support, or remote systems. It counts as a connected system for hacking.

  • Soaring: A flying vehicle. Once per scene, move anywhere within Far range, as long as there is enough open space to maneuver.

CHASES

During a chase, treat distance like normal range. On a successful driving roll, you can close or increase the distance by one range.

On a success with Hope, you can also create an opening, giving the next action against a target in the chase a +2 bonus.

On a failure with Fear, the GM may separate vehicles, introduce a hazard, damage the vehicle, block a route, attract attention, or put a passenger in danger.
