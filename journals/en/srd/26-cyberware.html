CYBERWARE AND COMPETENCY ACCESS

Cyberware is installed technology that changes what your body can do. Unlike weapons, armor, vehicles, or loot, Cyberware becomes part of you.

Each Cyberware has a Tier, a Cyber Cost, and a Feature. Your total Cyber Load is the sum of all installed Cyberware. Cosmetic or purely narrative implants usually have Cyber Cost 0. Some Cyberware also grants Competency Access.

CYBERWARE CHARACTER ADVANCEMENT

In Edgeheart, Cyberware replaces part of the normal advancement structure.

When characters grow in level and Tier, they still gain class features, Competency card options, Experiences, and narrative growth as normal. However, many numerical improvements that would normally come from advancement will instead come from installed Cyberware.

This means improvements such as increased Evasion, additional Hit Points, additional Stress, improved Traits, enhanced senses, integrated weapons, armor plating, and access to new Competencies are often tied to augmentation.

CYBERWARE TIERS

Cyberware quality and power is limited by Tier.

  • Tier 1: street-grade, black-market, civilian, gang-built, or basic corporate tech. It usually has Cyber Cost 0–1 and grants small features, utility, minor weapons, or Card Access.

  • Tier 2: military, professional, illegal, or high-grade corporate tech. It usually has Cyber Cost 1–2 and can improve HP, Stress, Evasion, Armor, Traits, or grant Half Access to a Competency.

  • Tier 3: elite, blacksite, prototype, or heavily modified tech. It usually has Cyber Cost 2–3 and can grant stronger combat features, Full Access to a Competency, major weaponware, or powerful defensive systems.

  • Tier 4: legendary, forbidden, experimental, Blackwall-touched, Redline-unstable, or Eidolon-linked. It usually has Cyber Cost 3+ and should feel like a major identity shift.

Cyberware Types

  • Utility Cyberware: Small tools and quality-of-life implants. Usually Cyber Cost 0–1.

  • Body Cyberware: Movement, senses, strength, defense, reflexes, organs, and prosthetics. Usually Cyber Cost 1–2.

  • Weaponware: Built-in weapons or weapon enhancers. These can count as Primary or Secondary Weapons if appropriate.

  • Competency Cyberware: Implants that grant Card, Half, or Full Access to a Competency.

  • Redline Cyberware: Illegal overclock systems that grant access to Redline or interact with controlled Cyberpsychosis.

  • Blackwall Cyberware: Corrupted implants, forbidden AI shards, hacked code, and Old Net relics that grant access to Blackwall.

COMPETENCY CYBERWARE

Cyberware can grant access to Competencies without making every character a full class.

  • Card Access: Gain access to one specific Competency card. This is common for utility implants.

  • Half Access: Gain access to cards of that Competency up to half your level, rounded up. This is usually Cyber Cost 2.

  • Full Access: Gain access to cards of that Competency up to your level. This is usually Cyber Cost 3 and should feel like a major identity shift.

  • Enhanced Access: You gain access to that Competency and a special benefit. This should usually come from rare Cyberware, Blackwall relics, Redline systems, or Eidolons.

In the module, access from cyberware appears on the Chrome tab of the Edgeheart Sheet and unlocks the cards in the system through the "Cyber Access" item.

CYBERWARE LIST

The GM can roll on this table, choose an implant, allow characters to purchase from this list, grant access based on Tier, or use these entries as inspiration to create unique rewards.

  • Common / Tier 1 (1d12 or 2d12): Street-grade implants, basic black-market chrome, civilian upgrades, gang modifications, utility ports, and standard clinic work.

  • Uncommon / Tier 2 (2d12 or 3d12): Professional augments, illegal upgrades, advanced prosthetics, black-market weaponware, and restricted corporate hardware.

  • Rare / Tier 3 (3d12 or 4d12): Blacksite cyberware, prototype systems, forbidden combat implants, Competency engines, elite military upgrades, and dangerous neural architecture.

  • Legendary / Tier 4 (4d12 or 5d12): Lost-tech relics, perfected prototypes, Blackwall artifacts, experimental hardware, Eidolon-link components, and treasures claimed from the most dangerous jobs in the New Dark Age.

If a Cyberware mentions Charges, those Charges belong only to that Cyberware and clear at the end of the scene unless the feature says otherwise.
