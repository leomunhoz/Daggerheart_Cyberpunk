Keep the following guidance in mind while running Edgeheart.

EVERY CONTRACT IS A STORY

Each contract should introduce a situation, pressure the characters, and force meaningful choices. Even a simple objective should reveal something about the city, the corps, the gangs, the client, or the mercenaries themselves.

Avoid making jobs feel like isolated tasks. Every contract should leave a mark.

THE CITY REACTS

The New Dark Age is not static. Corporations adapt. Gangs retaliate. Security learns. Clients lie. Rivals copy tactics. News spreads through broken channels and street rumor.

Let player actions shape future jobs. If the crew humiliates a corporation, that corporation remembers. If they save a district, someone there owes them.

CHROME IS POWER, NOT SAFETY

Characters in Edgeheart can become extremely powerful. They can hack networks, survive gunfire, pilot Eidolons, install illegal cyberware, and turn their bodies into weapons. Let them feel dangerous, but never let power feel clean.

Cyberware can malfunction. Cyber Load can build. Humanity can erode. A trusted implant can be traced. A weapon can become evidence. A perfect plan can collapse when the wrong door is analog. Threaten what their upgrades cannot protect.

TECHNOLOGY SHOULD FEEL PHYSICAL

Hacking is not abstract. When systems are breached, lights change, doors lock, weapons stutter, implants burn, alarms pulse, and people feel the consequences in their bodies.

Make technology visible at the table. A hack should not only be a number. It should change the space, the pressure, the options, and the danger.
